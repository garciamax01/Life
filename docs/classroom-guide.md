# One-period classroom guide

Students connect the lifestyle they want with the wages, trade-offs and next steps that could support it. Keep individual totals as planning choices; use the discussion to examine assumptions and reasoning.

1. **Predict.** Before researching prices, enter a guess for the annual salary needed. A nickname is enough. An unanswered prediction can stay blank.
2. **Build.** Complete the existing 13 steps. The default is the student's share of expenses. Optional household mode includes the other adult's entered expenses and wages.
3. **Check pay.** Test a wage on the results page. On Careers, expand **Use a real job posting** to record one posting or a teacher-provided job card. Record its title, employer/source, checked date, wage and paid schedule. Clearly identify an assumed schedule. A link, training requirements and benefits notes can also be included. Benefits notes do not automatically change healthcare costs.
4. **Revise.** The first completed plan is retained automatically. Use the direct edit buttons to change two choices, then compare original and current monthly costs, general savings targets, required wages and the remaining gap at the same selected main wage. If the household scope changes, discuss who each plan covers.
5. **Discuss one complication, if useful.** Open **Try an hours-change challenge**. It starts with 25% fewer paid hours and leaves the selected wage unchanged. Ask how the student would respond and whether transportation, shift times and care arrangements still work together.
6. **Reflect.** Select **Write my next step**. Three short answers identify the surprising expense, a change with a reason, and a concrete next step. Print the summary or save a progress file.

The starting-cash panel is optional. It adds the first month's full plan to the setup amounts entered, including deposits, furnishings, moving/connection fees and initial transportation cash. All four amounts, including any zeros, are needed for a complete estimate. These extras do not increase recurring monthly costs. A first month's paycheck may cover part of that cash target.

Protect time for the discussion. The app does not require extra wizard steps, a job search across many sites or completion of both optional panels. A teacher-selected posting keeps the wage check short. Example career wages remain scenarios; there are no preloaded listings presented as current offers.

## A simple job card to prepare

- Job title and employer/location
- Posting link or printed source
- Date the posting was checked
- Pay and whether it is hourly or annual
- Paid hours, paid weeks and whether the schedule is stated or assumed
- Required education, credential or training
- Health benefits, employee premium, waiting period and schedule eligibility, if stated

## Data and saving

The guide revision date is November 5, 2026. Local presets retain the teacher's Perris / Inland Empire ballpark figures; individual original quote dates were not supplied. The tax model remains explicitly historical 2024 because official sources could not be reached for current-year verification. A job's checked date is entered by the person reviewing the posting and does not represent automatic verification by the app.

Save progress downloads the nickname, original/current plans, job notes, setup estimates, challenge and reflection to a JSON file. Open progress restores them without an account, browser storage or uploads. Keep files appropriately on shared computers. Refreshing the page without saving clears the plan.
